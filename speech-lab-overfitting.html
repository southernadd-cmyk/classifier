Parliamentary Speech Lab

Can a neural network recognise the political party behind a speech? Train it on historical Commons debates, change its architecture and test your own words.

The labels describe the party of the MP who delivered the speech, not the ideology or truth of the speech. This is an educational experiment, not a political opinion detector.

About the data and its limitations

This is a historical dataset (1979–2019), and speeches are not a representative sample of everyone who supports a party. Pronouns, subjects, era, parliamentary conventions and government status can influence predictions. Direct party-name tokens are excluded, but other shortcuts remain possible. The small, sampled dataset is designed for classroom learning, not political profiling.

Speaker-held-out evaluation means no MP occurs in more than one of the training, validation and test partitions. Vocabulary is learned from the training partition only.
